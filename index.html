    Straight answers

    What people ask before signing

    How much is my remodel going to cost?

    Look at the ranges above for a starting point, then know that the real number comes from your actual house. Square footage, whether walls or plumbing move, what condition things are in behind the drywall, and the finishes you pick can swing a kitchen by twenty thousand dollars either direction. The walkthrough is free and the written estimate is itemized, so you'll see exactly where the money goes rather than one lump number.

    Can I live in the house during the work?

    For a single bathroom or a kitchen, usually yes — most people do. We seal off the work area, keep dust contained, and stage the work so you keep a functioning bathroom or a temporary kitchen setup. For a whole-home gut, generally no, and we'll say so honestly at the estimate rather than letting you find out in week two.

    How long will my kitchen be out of commission?

    A standard kitchen remodel runs about four to seven weeks on site, and the longest stretch is usually the gap between counter template and counter install, which is a fabricator lead time rather than us. We'll set up a temporary kitchen — fridge, microwave, and a sink you can reach — before demo starts.

    Do you handle permits?

    Most of what we do — drywall, texture, paint, flooring, trim, cabinets, tile — is finish work that doesn't require a permit. When a job does need one, or needs a licensed trade, that portion goes to the contractor who's licensed to pull it, and they pull it under their own license. We'll tell you up front which parts of your project fall into which bucket. Be cautious of anyone who offers to skip a permit to save you money — that becomes your problem when you sell the house.

    How do payments work?

    A deposit to schedule and order materials, progress payments tied to actual milestones, and a final payment after your punch list is complete and you've signed off. Nothing is due for work that hasn't happened yet. Anyone asking for the full amount up front should worry you.

    What if you find something bad behind the wall?

    On older homes it happens — rot, a leak nobody knew about, wiring that shouldn't be legal, or the previous owner's creative framing. We stop, photograph it, show you, and write a change order with a price before we touch it. You decide. Coming from the restoration side, we're better than most at spotting moisture problems before they become the whole project.

    Are you a licensed contractor?

    No, and here's the straight version. We're an interior finish company, not a general contractor. In Florida, the trades we work in — painting, drywall, flooring, cabinetry, tile, and interior finish work — aren't state-licensed contractor categories, and under a 2021 state law that took full effect in July 2025, counties can no longer require local licenses for them either. So there is no license to hold for this scope of work. We carry general liability insurance and you're welcome to see the certificate before we start.

    What that also means: we don't take on structural work, we don't move plumbing or electrical, and we don't do roofing or HVAC. Those need licensed contractors, and when your project includes them we bring one in rather than pretending we can handle it. If someone tells you they can do all of it themselves and they're not licensed, walk away.

    What won't you take on?

    Removing or altering load-bearing walls. Additions, garage conversions, and anything that changes the footprint of the house. Running new plumbing or electrical circuits. Roofing, HVAC, and window replacement. Anything that needs a permit pulled under a contractor's license. We'd rather tell you that in the first ten minutes than find out in week three — and we can usually point you to someone good for the parts we don't do.
