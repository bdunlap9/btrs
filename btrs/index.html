Why the first phone call matters

The damage is on a clock

Water that sat one hour and water that sat three days are two completely different jobs — different scope, different cost, and different risk to your family. Here's how it escalates.

Hour 1 Still spreading

Water travels sideways through carpet pad and under flooring, then wicks up drywall and into wall cavities you can't see. This is the cheapest hour of the entire job.

Hour 24 Materials start failing

Drywall swells and loses strength. Particleboard and MDF cabinet bases begin to break down. Furniture legs stain flooring. A musty smell shows up.

48–72 hr Microbial growth window

Mold can begin growing on damp organic material within roughly 24 to 48 hours. Clean water can also degrade into contaminated water the longer it sits.

Week 1 Mitigation becomes rebuild

Hardwood cups and buckles, trim and framing hold moisture, and what could have been a dry-out turns into demolition and reconstruction.

Timelines are general guidance from industry drying standards and EPA moisture guidance — every loss dries differently depending on materials, temperature, and humidity. Your actual conditions get measured on site, not guessed.

What we do

Get it dry. Keep it dry. Prove it's dry.

Mitigation is a measurable process, not a fan in the hallway and a handshake. Every step below produces a reading that goes in your file.

Water Extraction

Standing water comes out first — truck-mount and portable extraction, plus weighted extraction on carpet to pull water out of the pad instead of just the surface.

  • Standing water and saturated carpet pad
  • Contents moved, blocked, or protected
  • Source stopped or isolated before drying starts

Drying & Dehumidification

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers set by the numbers — room volume, wet square footage, and class of water loss — not by whatever was left on the truck.

  • Equipment counts calculated per IICRC S500
  • Air scrubbers to keep the air clean while it dries
  • Containment when the situation calls for it

Moisture Detection & Removal

Moisture meters and thermal imaging map how far the water actually traveled, so we open up what's wet and leave alone what isn't.

  • Moisture mapping of walls, floors, and cavities
  • Targeted removal instead of blanket demo
  • Daily readings until dry standard is reached

Prevent Future Damage

Drying the water is half of it. The other half is making sure the same thing doesn't happen again in eight months.

  • Antimicrobial treatment where appropriate
  • Plain-language cause explanation and photos
  • Repair recommendations and reconstruction referral

Water out, unsalvageable material out, contents protected. We take what's necessary and document why each piece came out.

Drying equipment set

Air movers, dehumidifiers, and air scrubbers placed to a calculated count for the size and class of the loss.

Daily monitoring

We come back and take readings every day, adjust equipment as the structure dries, and log it. You get progress, not silence.

Dry standard reached, equipment pulled

Equipment stays until the materials actually hit target moisture content — not until a set number of days runs out.

Documentation package

Photos, daily moisture logs, equipment records, and scope handed to you and your adjuster. Then we point you toward repairs.

Straight answers

Questions we get at 2 a.m.

How fast can you get here?

Call and we'll give you a real arrival window on the phone, not a promise we can't keep. We're based in Brevard County, so for most of the area it's a short drive. During a major storm event, response times stretch for everyone — call early rather than waiting to see if it dries on its own.

How long does drying take?

Most residential water losses dry in three to five days. It depends on how much water there was, what materials got wet, and how long it sat before we started. Hardwood, plaster, and concrete take considerably longer than drywall and carpet. We'll give you an estimate up front and update it daily based on actual readings.

Do I really need equipment running for days?

Yes, and this is where people get frustrated — the floor feels dry after a day, so the fans seem unnecessary. But the moisture that causes long-term problems is inside the drywall, the subfloor, and the framing. Meters tell us when it's actually dry. Pulling equipment early is the single most common reason a house needs to be dried twice.

Will my insurance cover this?

Sudden and accidental water damage — a pipe that bursts, a water heater that fails — is typically covered by homeowners policies. Long-term seepage, gradual leaks, and flooding from outside surface water usually are not, and flood damage generally requires separate flood insurance. We're not your carrier and can't approve a claim, but we can document the loss properly and tell you honestly what we're seeing.

Should I just rent a fan and do it myself?

For a small spill you caught immediately, sometimes yes. The problem is that box fans move air without removing moisture from it, so you can end up spreading humidity into rooms that were dry. If water got under flooring, into a wall cavity, or sat more than a few hours, get it measured before you decide. We'd rather tell you it's fine than see it again in six weeks as a mold job.

What do I do right now, before you arrive?

Shut off the water at the fixture or the main if you can do it safely. Stay out of standing water near outlets or electrical panels. Lift what you can off wet flooring, and put foil or blocks under furniture legs so stain doesn't transfer. Take photos of everything. Then leave the rest — that's what we're for.
